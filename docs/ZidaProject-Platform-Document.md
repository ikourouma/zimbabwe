## 1. What Was Built

Zimbabwe's investment proposition was, until now, distributed across documents, presentations, sector brochures and institutional memory. An investor wanting to understand what Zimbabwe offers had to assemble it, and an official wanting to know the current status of a project had to ask someone.

The platform replaces that with a single governed system. It is live at `https://zidaproject.com` and has been operated end to end — projects created, reviewed, approved and published; investors registered, verified and accredited; engagements opened and memoranda carried to execution.

The distinction between this document and the concept note is worth stating precisely, because it changes what the Government is being asked to consider. **The concept note asked Zimbabwe to imagine a platform. This document reports one that works.** Every capability described below can be demonstrated on request, from a browser, using a real account.

### What the platform does

| Capability | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| National project registry | Every investment opportunity as a structured record — sector, ministry, province, capital required, financing structure, readiness level, development goal alignment, financial indicators and supporting documents — rather than a page of prose |
| Governed publication | No project reaches the public registry without passing a review and approval sequence, with the publishing decision held by a named ZIDA officer |
| Ministry workspaces | Each ministry sees and manages its own portfolio, with national visibility but scoped authority |
| Tiered investor access | Public visitors, registered investors and accredited qualified investors each see a different depth of information, with the deepest tier requiring verification |
| Investor accreditation | A formal application, reviewed by ZIDA, that upgrades an investor's entitlements and is refused if the applicant's corporate identity is incomplete |
| Engagement pipeline | Investor interest in a named project, tracked from expression through government review to an approved engagement |
| Memoranda of understanding | Drafted jointly, approved by both the investor and the Government, finalised, circulated for signature and recorded as executed |
| Document vault | Controlled access to feasibility summaries, investor packs and project documentation, released by entitlement rather than by email |
| Complete audit trail | Every decision, transition, entitlement change and publication attributed to a named account and timestamped |
| Executive reporting | Portfolio composition, pipeline health, enquiry volume and governance activity as standing views rather than commissioned reports |

### What it deliberately is not

It is not a replacement for ZIDA, for any ministry, or for any existing government system. It does not hold regulatory authority, issue approvals, or make investment decisions. It is the layer on which Zimbabwe's institutions do those things visibly, in sequence, and with a record.

## 2. Alignment With Zimbabwe's National Objectives

Zimbabwe's development architecture sets out where the country intends to be. The platform is an instrument for closing the distance between that intent and its visible execution.

| National objective | What it requires | What the platform contributes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vision 2030** — upper-middle-income status | Sustained investment inflow into productive sectors | A credible, current, investor-facing view of the national opportunity pipeline, with the readiness and financial detail investment committees actually require |
| **National Development Strategy 2** (2026–2030) | Coordinated delivery across ministries against measurable priorities | Every project classified against a strategic pillar and a beneficiary ministry, making the portfolio's alignment with national priorities visible as data rather than assertion |
| **Smart Zimbabwe 2030** | Digitally enabled public institutions and services | A working government platform in production use, with role-based access, workflow governance and audit — the pattern subsequent systems can follow |
| **ZIDA's statutory mandate** | Investment promotion, facilitation and aftercare in one agency | The operational console through which ZIDA publishes opportunities, accredits investors, routes enquiries and carries agreements to signature |
| **Ease of doing business** | Reduced friction between investor interest and institutional response | A single entry point where an investor can discover, register, qualify, engage and reach the right desk without knowing which office to write to |
| **Institutional accountability** | Traceable decisions in public administration | Every act of delegated authority recorded against the officer who exercised it |

### The strategic argument in one paragraph

Zimbabwe does not have an investment opportunity problem. The pipeline exists, the sectors are identified, and the projects are real. What has been missing is the connective infrastructure that lets an investor in Johannesburg, Dubai or Washington evaluate that pipeline with the same confidence as a domestic one, and that lets Government see, coordinate and govern the response. That is what this platform supplies, and it supplies it now rather than as a programme of work.

> **A note on sequencing**
> This platform was deliberately built as the first visible layer rather than as a comprehensive digital government programme. The reasoning is that institutional confidence in digital transformation is earned by delivery, not by roadmaps. A working investment platform that ministries use every week is a stronger foundation for the next system than a strategy document about all of them.

## 3. The Governance Model

The most important design decision in this platform is that **nothing significant rests on one person's authority.** That principle is worth examining, because it is the reason the platform can be trusted with a national registry.

### The project lifecycle

A project moves through a defined sequence, and each transition is an act by a named officer.

| State | What it means | Who can move it forward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draft | Being prepared; not visible outside its owner | Its author |
| Submitted for review | The mandatory template is complete and the record has been put forward | The submitting ministry or officer |
| Under review | A reviewer is validating completeness, classification and institutional alignment | Government Reviewer, ZIDA Admin |
| Changes requested | Returned with reviewer notes for correction | Returns to the author |
| Approved | Validated and cleared, but not yet public | Government Reviewer, ZIDA Admin |
| **Published** | On the national registry and visible to investors | **ZIDA Admin only** |
| Archived | No longer active; the terminal state | ZIDA Admin, Platform Manager |

Two properties of this sequence matter more than the sequence itself.

**Approval and publication are separated.** The officer who validates a project is not necessarily the officer who publishes it, and publication — the moment the registry becomes externally visible and the Government is, in effect, making a representation to the market — is held by ZIDA alone. No ministry can publish its own project.

**There is no deletion.** Nothing on this platform can be destroyed. Archived is the terminal state, and archived records remain in the audit trail. A registry from which entries can vanish is not a registry.

### Where authority is deliberately withheld

| Act | Withheld from | Held by | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Publishing a project | Ministries, reviewers | ZIDA Admin | Publication is a national representation, not a departmental one |
| Unpublishing a project | ZIDA Admin | Platform Manager | Withdrawing something from the national registry is a heavier act than placing it there, and must not be quietly reversible |
| Changing the classification scheme | Everyone except the owner tier | Platform Manager | Editing a sector or ministry retroactively changes the meaning of every record already filed against it |
| Creating an administrator | ZIDA Admin | Platform Manager | An administrator cannot expand the set of administrators; that is the standard route to privilege escalation |
| Moving a project outside the workflow | Everyone except the owner tier | Platform Manager | The escape hatch from governance belongs to one tier, is recorded with a stated reason, and is itself reversible |

### The two-sided memorandum

When an engagement is approved and a memorandum of understanding is drafted, it cannot proceed on one signature of intent. The investor approves; the Government approves; only when both approvals are recorded does the document reach a state where it can be finalised. If either party requests a change, **both approvals are cleared** — so neither side's earlier approval carries over onto text they have not read.

This is the platform's governance philosophy in miniature: consequential acts require more than one party, and the system enforces it rather than relying on procedure.

## 4. Institutional Coordination and the Six Roles

The platform implements six roles. Each corresponds to a real institutional position, and the boundaries between them are the platform's model of how Zimbabwe's investment architecture is meant to work.

| Role | Institutional position | Scope of authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registered Investor | An investor who has created an account | Discovery and expression of interest |
| Qualified Investor | An investor ZIDA has verified and accredited | Financial indicators, gated documents, engagements and memoranda |
| Government Reviewer | A national-level officer validating projects across all ministries | Review through to approved; cannot publish |
| Ministry Official | A beneficiary ministry's desk | Full authority over its own portfolio; visibility of the national picture |
| ZIDA Admin | The agency's operational centre | Publication, accreditation, execution of agreements |
| Platform Manager | The platform owner tier | Configuration, entitlements, the classification scheme, and reversal |

### Ministry scoping, stated precisely

A Ministry Official can see the national portfolio. What they cannot do is act on another ministry's projects. This distinction is deliberate and was verified rather than assumed: a ministry officer attempting to edit a project belonging to another ministry is refused by the system, not merely discouraged by the interface.

The effect is that ministries gain national visibility — they can see how their sector sits within the whole — without the platform becoming a place where one ministry can alter another's commitments. A project's beneficiary ministry can only be changed through an association request decided by ZIDA.

### The role the concept note did not anticipate

Concept Note v0.3 described ten personas. Implementation surfaced a need the note had not: a national-level reviewer, tied to no single ministry and holding no publication authority, to carry the validation workload. That is the Government Reviewer role.

It is a substantial position — it sees every project at every status nationally, edits content during review, and approves memoranda on the Government's behalf. Because it was not in the note, it has not yet been formally reviewed by ZIDA, and **who in Zimbabwe's structure should hold it is an open question this document puts to Government.**

## 5. From Visitor to Verified Investor

The platform is not a brochure with a contact form. It is a conversion pathway, and each step exchanges information for access under terms the Government sets.

| Tier | What they can see | How they advance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public visitor | Published projects, sector and pillar structure, national overview — no financial indicators | Register |
| Registered investor | Full project detail, ministry alignment, development goal mapping — still no financial indicators or documents | Apply for accreditation |
| Qualified investor | Financial indicators, feasibility summaries, investor packs, engagements, memoranda | — |

### The accreditation gate

Advancing to qualified status is a decision ZIDA makes, not a form an investor completes. The applicant submits their organisation, business registration identifier, headquarters address, corporate website and stated investment interest. A ZIDA Admin reviews the dossier and approves, declines, or requests changes — and every outcome is communicated to the applicant automatically and recorded against the deciding officer.

One guard is worth highlighting to an executive audience. **Approval is refused by the system if the applicant's corporate identity is incomplete.** There is no path to qualified status that bypasses this check, including direct promotion by an administrator. An unverifiable entity cannot be granted access to Zimbabwe's project financials by administrative shortcut, however senior the administrator.

### What this produces for Zimbabwe

Each tier transition is a structured, attributable record. The result is that ZIDA can answer questions it previously could not: which sectors attract the most registered interest, how many verified investors are active, which projects convert interest into engagement, and where interest is expressed but never followed up. That is the beginning of genuine investment intelligence, and it is a by-product of the workflow rather than a separate reporting exercise.

## 6. Economic Intelligence

The platform's analytical value comes from the fact that its data is generated by operations rather than collected for reporting. Nobody compiles it; it accumulates.

| What is measured | What it tells Government |
| --- | --- |
| Portfolio composition by sector, pillar, ministry and province | Whether the pipeline reflects national priorities or has drifted from them |
| Project readiness distribution | How much of the portfolio is genuinely investable now versus at concept stage |
| Registered and qualified investor volume | Whether the market is engaging, and at what depth |
| Enquiry volume and routing | Which sectors and which projects generate demand |
| Engagement conversion | Where interest becomes a real transaction, and where it stalls |
| Review queue depth and age | Whether institutional response is keeping pace with market interest |
| Governance activity | Who is deciding what, and how quickly |

The last two are the ones most often absent from investment promotion. A queue that is visible is a queue that gets worked. When the time a project spends awaiting review is a number on a dashboard rather than an impression, institutional performance becomes manageable.

## 7. Confidentiality, Security and the Audit Trail

### The confidentiality undertaking

Before any non-staff account can reach the platform's protected content, it must accept a confidentiality undertaking. This is a versioned clickwrap agreement with an audit record: who accepted, which version, and when. It is not dismissible, and there is no route past it.

This matters because the platform carries pre-publication project data, financial indicators, feasibility material and, in the memorandum workflow, the terms of agreements under negotiation. Access to that material is granted on recorded terms.

### The audit trail

Every consequential act is recorded: project transitions, publication decisions, entitlement and role changes, accreditation outcomes, memorandum approvals, and any use of the platform-owner override. Each record carries the actor, the action, the affected record, and the time.

The trail is queryable and filterable by the Platform Manager tier. Role changes record both the previous and the new role, so an account's entitlement history is fully reconstructible — which is the property an auditor actually needs.

> **An assurance question for ZIDA**
> The audit trail's coverage was designed against ordinary institutional expectations, not against ZIDA's specific assurance requirements. If Zimbabwe's auditors expect a category of event that is not currently recorded, that is far easier to add now than after a year of records exists in a shape that omits it.

### Access control

Authorisation is enforced at the server, not in the interface. A role that cannot perform an act is refused when it attempts the act, not merely presented with a screen that omits the button. This distinction is the difference between an interface that looks governed and a system that is.

## 8. Data Sovereignty and Intellectual Property

This section states the ownership model plainly, because it is the question most likely to be asked in the room and least well served by ambiguity.

### What Zimbabwe owns and controls

The Government of Zimbabwe, ZIDA, ministries, agencies and designated public institutions retain ownership and authority over:

- All official data entered into the platform
- All investment project information, including financial indicators and readiness assessments
- All national content, policy material and institutional records
- All documents uploaded to the vault
- Every institutional approval and publication decision
- The determination of what is published, to whom, and at what level of visibility

No project appears on the national registry except by a Zimbabwean officer's decision. Afronovation does not publish, approve, accredit, or decide anything on Zimbabwe's behalf.

### What Afronovation owns

Afronovation owns and retains, as proprietary intellectual property:

- The software platform and its source code
- The technical architecture, data model and design system
- The reusable modules, workflow engine and entitlement framework
- The implementation methodology
- Any AI-enabled components
- The super-admin configuration layer

The platform is delivered under a software-as-a-service licensing, implementation and support model. Zimbabwe receives configured access, publishing permissions, review workflows, dashboards and institutional workspaces under agreed entitlements.

### Why this model serves Zimbabwe

The alternative — a bespoke system built and handed over — appears to offer more control and in practice offers less. A bespoke system stops improving on the day it is delivered, its maintenance becomes a permanent budget line, and its institutional knowledge leaves with the vendor's team. A licensed platform continues to be developed, inherits improvements built for every other deployment, and carries its implementation risk on the vendor's side rather than the Government's.

What Zimbabwe must retain is authority over its data and its decisions. That is retained in full. What Zimbabwe does not need to own is the engineering.

> **The custody question**
> The Platform Manager tier is currently held by Afronovation as the implementation partner. That is normal during a pilot and requires a deliberate decision before production. Whether ZIDA takes custody, whether Afronovation retains it under a written arrangement with defined scope, or whether both hold accounts, is a governance decision for Government. It is raised here rather than left to be settled by default.

## 9. What Is Live, What Is Deferred

Credibility requires stating what was not built as clearly as what was.

### Live and demonstrable

Six of the concept note's ten personas are implemented. The project registry, governance workflow, publication control, ministry scoping, tiered investor access, accreditation, engagements, memoranda, document vault, audit trail, executive reporting, communication hub and notification system are all in production use.

### Deferred, with the reason

| Concept note persona | Status | What is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diaspora Investor | Deferred | A diaspora investor registers and invests today like any other. What is absent is the tailored discovery experience and the structured diaspora pipeline the note describes — not the ability to participate |
| Project Owner | Deferred | The entity delivering a project cannot currently post its own updates or documents. That work is done by the ministry on the sponsor's behalf. **This is the most operationally significant gap of the three** |
| Embassy Investment Desk | Deferred | Embassy staff would today require a Government Reviewer account, which grants national scope rather than the market-specific view the note envisages |

None of these is blocked by anything. Each was scoped out to reach a working pilot, and each can be added. Government's view on which matters most, and whether any must exist before production, is requested in section 12.

## 10. The Pathway Beyond Investment Promotion

Concept Note v0.3 positioned this platform as the first visible layer of a broader national digital acceleration pathway. That framing has now been tested by delivery, and it holds — but the argument is stronger than it was, because the pattern is no longer hypothetical.

What this platform demonstrates, and what any subsequent government system would inherit, is a working implementation of: role-based institutional access, multi-stage approval workflows with separation of authority, complete audit trails over acts of delegated power, tiered public and private information disclosure, document custody with entitlement-based release, and two-party agreement execution.

Those are not investment-promotion features. They are the foundational patterns of digital government. Building them once and reusing them is the difference between a modernisation programme and a sequence of unrelated procurements.

### Institutional email identity

One adjacent observation, surfaced by the pilot and recorded here because the moment to raise it is while account structure is still being decided.

Every account on this platform is an email address, and that address is what the audit trail records against every project approved, published or accredited. Where those accounts sit on personal or commercial mailboxes, the durability of the institutional record depends on a mailbox no institution controls. When an officer transfers or leaves, the record of their decisions remains; their access does not necessarily follow.

A government email management system — secure, centrally administered `.gov.zw` addresses for officials — would make institutional identity verifiable rather than asserted. The platform could then require an official address for government roles, infer an officer's ministry from their address rather than assigning it by hand, and tie platform access to employment status so that a departure withdraws access automatically.

This is not in scope for this pilot and is not a dependency. It is raised because it is exactly the kind of shared foundation that every government system built after this one would inherit, and because aligning to it deliberately now is a small change where retrofitting later is a migration.

## 11. What Success Looks Like, and How It Is Measured

The measures below are stated so that the platform can be held to them.

| Measure | Test |
| --- | --- |
| Investor comprehension | An investor unfamiliar with Zimbabwe can understand the national opportunity landscape within minutes of arriving |
| Discoverability | Any project can be found by sector, strategic pillar, development goal, ministry, province, financing type and readiness level |
| Lead capture | Investor interest becomes a structured, attributable record rather than an email |
| Governance integrity | Every published project has passed review and approval, and the officer who published it is named |
| Institutional coordination | Ministries can see their portfolio's place in the national picture without being able to alter another ministry's records |
| Response performance | Queue depth and age are visible, so institutional responsiveness is managed rather than assumed |
| Auditability | Any decision on the platform can be reconstructed, with its actor and its time |

## 12. Decisions Requested of Government

The platform is built. What remains are decisions that are properly Zimbabwe's, and that are least expensive to make now.

| # | Decision | Why it matters now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Should publication require one ZIDA officer or two? | It currently requires one. Publication is the point at which the registry becomes a public representation |
| 2 | Who should hold the Platform Manager tier in production? | An implementation partner holding the highest tier is normal in a pilot and unusual after handover |
| 3 | Should use of the platform-owner override be reported to ZIDA routinely? | It is currently recorded and discoverable, which is not the same as reported |
| 4 | Should ZIDA be able to unpublish a project directly? | That currently requires the Platform Manager |
| 5 | Should a Ministry Official have a say in whether an engagement on their own project proceeds? | They currently have visibility but no decision |
| 6 | Which deferred persona matters most, and must any exist before production? | Our reading is that Project Owner carries the most operational weight |
| 7 | Who in Zimbabwe's structure should hold the Government Reviewer role? | The role was not in the concept note and has not been formally reviewed |
| 8 | Should government accounts be required to use official government addresses? | A small change today; a migration later |
| 9 | Is the audit trail's coverage sufficient for ZIDA's auditors? | Far easier to extend before a year of records exists in the current shape |
| 10 | On what commercial and governance terms does this move to production? | The pilot has demonstrated the product; the operating model is the remaining question |

### The proposed next step

A working session with ZIDA leadership and the relevant institutional focal points to settle the ten decisions above, confirm the first tranche of projects for validated publication, and agree the production operating model, licensing arrangement and support terms.

> **Closing position**
> Zimbabwe retains full authority over its national content, official data, project information, institutional priorities and policy decisions. Afronovation provides the proprietary platform, the implementation capability and the continuing engineering required to keep it current. The platform begins as a governed investment intelligence layer and can extend, through the same patterns, into a broader national digital acceleration pathway.
