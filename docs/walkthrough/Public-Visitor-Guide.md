## 1. Who This Persona Is

The Public Visitor is anyone who arrives at the platform without an account: a prospective investor doing early research, a journalist, a development partner, a Zimbabwean citizen, or a government counterpart from another country.

This tier deserves more attention than it usually gets in a review, for a simple reason. It is the only tier every other persona passes through. An investor who never gets past the public site never becomes a registered investor, and never becomes a qualified one. Everything downstream depends on this layer being credible.

The design problem it solves is a real tension. Publish too little and the platform looks like a brochure with nothing behind it. Publish too much and commercially sensitive material is handed to anonymous visitors. The resolution here is to be **fully open about what exists and completely closed about what it is worth**: every published project is visible with its full non-financial description, and no financial figure is disclosed to anyone who has not been verified.

> **Concept note lineage**
> Section 10 of Concept Note v0.3 defines this persona as: *"Browse public project summaries, read national investment information, register interest."* All three are live.

## 2. The Public Site

### Home

![The public entry point, summarising Zimbabwe's investment opportunity and the governed workflow behind every published project.](docs/screenshots/public/home.png)

The first thing a visitor meets is a statement of provenance. Above the navigation sits a standing notice that the demonstration data is seeded from the ZIDA 2025 Projects deck and pending official validation. Beneath it are the Republic of Zimbabwe wordmark and a navigation bar running Opportunity, Platform Concept, Strategic Pillars, Priority Sectors, Project Registry, Afronovation and Contact, with Sign in and a green Register button to the right. The hero reads *Discover governed, searchable investment opportunities across Zimbabwe*.

Two things are settled in the first few seconds. The page is presented under national identity rather than a vendor's, and it says what the data is before anyone has to ask. A project search field and an English/French toggle sit above everything else, which tells you the assumed audience is international. The primary action offered is Explore Projects, not Register — the site sets out to earn the account rather than demand it at the door.

### National Opportunity

![Why Zimbabwe, why now, and the case for a governed digital investment catalogue.](docs/screenshots/public/opportunity.png)

Here the investment case is stated with figures attached. Under the heading *Zimbabwe's Investment Inflection Point*, a short paragraph cites growth of 6.6% in 2025 rising to a projected 8.5% in 2026, a resurgent mining sector, record foreign currency earnings, and a diaspora sending home more than $2.45 billion a year. Four cards beneath carry the platform's own counts: 38 catalogue projects, 8 sectors, 26 published registry entries and 10 provinces of geographic coverage.

Macroeconomic claims are common in investment promotion. Placing them directly above a live count of what actually sits on the registry behind them is less common. A reader who notes *26 published* can move to the registry and read all twenty-six, which converts the narrative from assertion into something checkable in the next click. That link between the national story and the inventory supporting it is the difference between a country describing momentum and a country evidencing it.

### Platform Concept

![How the registry, governance workflow and entitlement tiers fit together.](docs/screenshots/public/platform.png)

Platform Overview sets out the architecture in the visitor's own view. The opening line commits to strengthening investment visibility, project discovery, investor engagement and institutional coordination without replacing ZIDA or government systems. A grid of cards follows: Project Registry, annotated 38 projects and 26 published; Governance Workflow, which spells out draft, review, approve, publish and adds that there is no direct publishing from draft; and Persona & Entitlements, naming the public, registered, qualified investor and admin levels.

Publishing the operating model is a commitment rather than a description. Once a visitor has read that nothing moves from draft to published without review, the agency can be held to that rule by an investor, a journalist or a ministry alike. The undertaking not to replace ZIDA or government systems answers the first question most institutions put to a new platform, which is what it displaces. The answer given here, in public, is nothing.

This page is worth reviewing carefully, because it is where the platform explains its own governance model to the public. If the description here does not match how ZIDA intends to operate, that is a discrepancy visible to everyone.

### Strategic Pillars

![The national transformation pillars each project is classified against.](docs/screenshots/public/strategic-alignment.png)

Strategic Pillars & Policy Alignment explains the classification every project carries. Projects map to one or more of eleven universal pillars, seventeen SDGs and beneficiary ministries, and the introduction notes plainly that ministry mappings are demonstration placeholders pending official validation. A numbered index of pillars runs down the left, from agriculture and food security through diaspora and trade to digital infrastructure. The panel beside it opens Pillar 09, Investment Attraction & Private Sector Growth, marked Active, with its strategic mandate and target outcomes for investor facilitation, licensing and One-Stop Investment Services Centre capacity.

For a development finance institution or a mandate-bound fund, the first question asked of any pipeline is which national objective it serves. This page answers before it is asked, and at the level of the individual pillar rather than in general terms: the mandate, the target outcomes, and the policy frameworks each pillar advances. It also marks its own provisional data as provisional, which is a small discipline that does considerable work. A reader who finds a caveat where one is warranted will trust the uncaveated material more.

### Priority Sectors

![Economic sectors with their associated project pipelines.](docs/screenshots/public/sectors.png)

The Sector Explorer divides the registry eight ways. Each card names a sector, states how many projects are published within it, gives the number of subsectors and provinces it touches, and lists the strategic pillar tags it carries. Agriculture shows six published across five provinces, ICT shows five across three, and Health shows one. Two links close every card: one into the sector's own detail page, the other straight into the registry already filtered to that sector.

Institutional capital arrives with a sector mandate, and this is the view that lets a fund find its own ground in under a minute. The counts are the telling part. Health carrying a single published project is neither concealed nor padded, and the same page that promotes the sectors with depth also shows a ZIDA officer precisely where the national pipeline is thin. One page therefore serves two purposes: an investor's shortlist and an origination brief for government.

### Project Registry

![The searchable catalogue of published opportunities. Financial indicators are withheld at this tier.](docs/screenshots/public/projects.png)

The registry itself, and the only page in this sequence rendered on a light background. A count of twenty-six projects sits above a search field that accepts title, location or project owner, next to a Filters control and a Save Search option. Every card carries a sector tag, a title, a short description, strategic pillar tags, numbered SDG badges, the provinces involved, the named owner and a View opportunity button. Goromonzi Agro Processing Industrial Park, a Special Economic Zone across 1,718 hectares, sits beside Mossfield's 15,000-hectare crop venture and a macadamia and avocado plantation expansion.

Read the cards again for what is absent: no capital requirement, no projected return, no payback period. What is present is everything needed to judge whether a project merits pursuit — where it is, who owns it, what it involves, and which national objectives it advances. Naming Goromonzi Agro Industrial Company and Mossfield Farms in public is itself a form of accountability, because a registry with identified counterparties is one whose entries can be independently verified.

This is the substance of the platform. Every project here has been through the governance workflow: created by a ministry, ZIDA or a verified investor, reviewed, approved, and published by ZIDA. Nothing appears on this page by any other route.

You can browse and filter freely. What you will not find is a single capital figure or projected return.

### Investor Journey

![The opening of the investor journey, introducing a governed pathway from registry browsing to strategic partnership.](docs/screenshots/public/investor-journey.png)

*From Discovery to Strategic Partnership* describes the route an institution takes through the platform. The opening text sets out a governed four-step pathway connecting a browse of the ZIDA project registry to a qualified investment conversation, with entitlements that expand as the visitor registers and then qualifies. An Engagement Pathway section below restates the same framing for institutional investors approaching the ZIDA pipeline. The four steps themselves sit further down the page, beyond this view.

Stating the pathway before anyone walks it removes the sense that access is arbitrary. An investment committee can establish, before committing time or fees, exactly what is available at each tier and what is required to reach the next one — a materially different proposition from a site that reveals its restrictions only when a document refuses to open. Predictable escalation is what makes gated disclosure acceptable to serious counterparties instead of merely obstructive.

### Strategic Partnerships

![The first step of the executive gateway, where a counterparty states its engagement type.](docs/screenshots/public/strategic-partnerships.png)

Labelled an executive gateway, *Strategic Partnerships & Inquiries* is a three-step form for counterparties with a specific mandate. The introduction names its intended users — investors, government and DFI counterparts, strategic or technical partners — and directs anyone with a general or press question to the standard contact form instead. A numbered indicator marks step one of three, *who you are and why*, which opens on engagement type: Investor, covering institutional, private capital and diaspora mandates, and Government / DFI, covering ministries, agencies and development finance institutions.

Segmenting at the first field rather than the last is what stops a serious approach being lost among general correspondence. A sovereign fund and a student research request warrant different destinations, different response times and different handling, and this page makes that determination before a line of the message has been written. ZIDA receives an enquiries queue already sorted by counterparty type; the counterparty is read by someone with the standing to respond.

### National Profile

![Country overview and macroeconomic context.](docs/screenshots/public/zimbabwe.png)

The national profile opens with the coat of arms and a short statement of position: diversified investment opportunity across ten provinces and eight priority economic sectors, curated from the ZIDA 2025 Projects deck and presented as a governed, investor-facing registry. Four figures follow — 32 catalogue projects described as the ZIDA seed pipeline, 8 economic sectors, 26 published investor-facing entries and 11 strategic pillars. The catalogue figure is lower than the 38 carried on National Opportunity and Platform Concept because the two counts are labelled differently: this page reports the seed pipeline drawn from the 2025 deck, while those pages report every ZIDA catalogue entry the platform holds. The published figure of 26 is identical on all three. A Back to Gateway link sits above the title.

A country page that leads with sovereign identity and then immediately quantifies itself does more useful work than a promotional overview. It gives a first-time reader the shape of the offer — how wide the sector spread is, how much of the pipeline has cleared review, how many provinces are represented — in four figures that can each be tested elsewhere on the site. For an investor forming a first view of Zimbabwe, that is a faster and more candid orientation than narrative alone provides.

### Contact

![General enquiries, with a reason-for-contact field that drives routing and segmentation.](docs/screenshots/public/contact.png)

*Talk to the platform team* pairs a form with three published commitments: investment and partnership enquiries acknowledged within one business day, routing determined by the stated reason for contact rather than a shared inbox, and confidentiality limiting details to the relevant Afronovation and ZIDA liaison team. The form asks for the reason for contact first, then name and email as required fields, followed by an organisation field, a phone number defaulting to the +263 dialling code, and a message box above the send button.

Placing the routing field above the visitor's own name reveals what the page is built for, which is getting the enquiry to the right desk rather than merely capturing it. The response standard carries comparable weight. One business day is a commitment that can be measured, and missed, and an agency willing to print one in public is signalling that enquiries are worked rather than accumulated. The +263 default with international dialling accommodates both domestic and diaspora contact without configuration.

### Frequently Asked Questions

![The opening section of the FAQ, on what the platform is and whose authority it carries.](docs/screenshots/public/faq.png)

The FAQ opens on *About the Platform* with its first two answers already expanded. The first defines the platform as a governed digital investment intelligence system that strengthens investment visibility, project discovery, investor engagement and institutional coordination, transforming ZIDA's static 2025 catalogue into a searchable, filterable registry. The second asks whether this is an official ZIDA or Government of Zimbabwe platform, and answers no: it is a proprietary Afronovation system configured for Zimbabwe, with a link through to the Governance & Legal page for the full disclaimer.

That second answer is the most consequential sentence on the public site. Rather than borrowing the authority of the state, the platform states its limits and points to where formal authority actually rests, noting that ZIDA and government systems remain the authoritative source for investment promotion, licensing and regulatory processes. This protects the Government from having a private system mistaken for an official licensing channel, and protects investors from treating a registry entry as an approval it is not.

### Afronovation

![The implementation partner and platform owner.](docs/screenshots/public/about-afronovation.png)

This page identifies who operates the platform. A pull quote names Afronovation as owner, operator and technology partner, delivering proprietary SaaS infrastructure configured for Zimbabwe's investment promotion ecosystem. Six cards set out what that involves: governed registry, investor engagement covering registration and lead capture, governance workflow with review and approval before publication and a full audit trail, platform admin control over taxonomies and entitlements, data sovereignty, and a deployment pathway beginning with validation.

Naming the operator in public is a diligence requirement rather than a courtesy. Anyone assessing the platform on the Government's behalf can see who is accountable for it, what the company undertakes to do, and — in the data sovereignty card — where ownership of ZIDA and government data is asserted to rest. A registry that carries the national coat of arms needs a visible answer to the question of who runs it, and that answer is one click from the navigation on every public page.

### Legal

![The ZIDA and Government disclaimer, set above the opening of the terms of service.](docs/screenshots/public/legal.png)

*Platform Policies & Disclaimers* gathers the governing terms in one place. The lead item, given its own highlighted panel, is the ZIDA & Government Disclaimer: the platform is a proprietary Afronovation system, project data derives from the ZIDA 2025 Projects deck for executive review and investor discovery, and all catalogue entries, ministry mappings and capital estimates are pending official validation by designated Zimbabwean authorities. The terms of service begin immediately beneath, opening with acceptance of terms.

Disclosure of this kind is usually written to be found only by those who go looking for it. Setting the validation status in the first panel of the legal page, in the same terms as the banner carried across every public page, means no investor can reach a decision without having seen it. That consistency between a demonstration notice and a legal instrument is a modest discipline, but it is the kind that indicates the platform's claims about itself are actively managed.

### Sign In and Create Account

![Where every persona begins. Where you land afterwards depends on your account's role.](docs/screenshots/public/sign-in.png)

Sign in is a split screen, with the credential panel on the right and the explanation on the left. Under a secure access gateway label, the heading reads *Sign In to Your Governed Profile*, and the text names who uses this door: returning investors, ministry stakeholders and platform administrators. Three notes beneath cover role-based entitlements across the registered, qualified, government and admin tiers, the governed Deal Room, and institutional consoles for ZIDA review and super-admin taxonomy control. The form asks only for an email address and a password, with a Create an account link beneath the Sign In button. A footnote at the foot of the left-hand panel addresses anyone new to the platform, inviting them to create an account and browse the national project registry.

One entrance serves every persona on the platform, from a diaspora investor to a super administrator, and the account's role decides what appears next. For a system with this many tiers that is a meaningful simplification: no separate government portal to locate, no second address to circulate, and no prospect of a minister or an investor arriving at the wrong front door. The left-hand panel also sets expectations before credentials are entered, so a first-time user knows which tier they are joining.

![Self-registration, which creates a Registered Investor account.](docs/screenshots/public/sign-up.png)

Registration asks four things: full name, email, password and confirmation. The panel alongside explains what the account provides — immediate access to the ZIDA project registry, strategic alignment mapping across pillars, SDGs and sector intelligence, and a route to the Deal Room and capital estimates by way of a Strategic Partnership enquiry submitted afterwards. A footnote confirms the account is created instantly at the Registered tier, and states that financial indicators and project documents are released once ZIDA has verified the applicant's organisation. Two exits close the panel: sign in for those already holding an account, and a direct link to begin a Strategic Partnership enquiry.

This is the conversion step, and it is deliberately inexpensive. Nothing is requested here that a genuine prospect would hesitate over, and because the tier is granted immediately the visitor browses rather than waits on an approval. Verification is deferred to the point where it earns its cost, which is the request for financial detail. The second exit matters as much, since an institution that ought to apply as an organisation is offered that route rather than funnelled through a personal registration.

## 4. What You Cannot Do, and Why

| Not available | Why | Available at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capital requirements, projected returns, payback periods | Commercially sensitive; released only to verified investors | Qualified Investor |
| Feasibility studies, investor packs and other documents | Same, and downloads are recorded against a verified identity | Qualified Investor |
| Saving projects to a shortlist | Requires an account | Registered Investor |
| Contacting government about a specific project | Requires a verified counterparty | Qualified Investor |
| Seeing unpublished projects | Drafts and submissions are internal to government and their originators | Government roles |

> **The line the platform draws**
> Everything about *what exists* is public. Everything about *what it is worth* requires verification. That is why you can read a project's full description, sector, location, strategic alignment and beneficiary ministry, but not one financial figure.

## 5. Step-by-Step Processes

### Process A — Explore the national opportunity

1. Start at `https://zidaproject.com`.
2. Read **National Opportunity** at `/opportunity` for the investment case.
3. Read **Platform Concept** at `/platform` to understand how projects reach the registry.
4. Review **Strategic Pillars** at `/strategic-alignment` — every project is classified against these.
5. Browse **Priority Sectors** at `/sectors`.

### Process B — Browse the project registry

1. Go to `https://zidaproject.com/projects`.
2. Filter by sector, province or strategic pillar.
3. Open a project and read its description, location, sector, strategic alignment, development goals and beneficiary ministry.
4. Note what is not shown. Where financial indicators would be, the platform tells you they require verification rather than leaving a blank.

> **What to check**
> Is the level of public disclosure right? Too little and serious investors will not engage; too much and sensitive material is exposed. This is a judgement for ZIDA rather than a technical setting, and section 7 is where to record it.

### Process C — Submit a general enquiry

1. Go to `https://zidaproject.com/contact`.
2. Complete your details and select a **reason for contact**. This field routes your enquiry, so it determines who reads it.
3. Submit.

Your enquiry reaches ZIDA's inquiries queue, where staff triage it alongside qualified-investor applications.

### Process D — Apply as an institutional partner

Use this if you represent an institution and want to be verified without first creating an account and applying from inside.

1. Go to `https://zidaproject.com/strategic-partnerships`.
2. Complete the application: your organisation, your mandate, and the nature of the partnership you envisage.
3. Submit.

ZIDA reviews it in the same queue as applications raised from inside the platform.

### Process E — Create an account

1. Go to `https://zidaproject.com/auth/sign-up`.
2. Enter your name, email address and a password.
3. You are signed in immediately as a **Registered Investor** and arrive at `https://zidaproject.com/deal-room`.

From here the Registered Investor guide takes over. In short: you gain a project shortlist, a company profile, and the ability to apply for qualified status.

## 6. The Two Ways In

There are two routes from public visitor to verified investor, and they suit different people.

| Route | Start | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Create an account, then apply** | `/auth/sign-up` | An individual investor or a small team. You get a working shortlist immediately and apply when ready. |
| **Apply as an institution** | `/strategic-partnerships` | A fund, corporate or development institution, where the application is an organisational act rather than a personal one. |

Both arrive in the same ZIDA queue and both result in a Qualified Investor account on approval. The difference is where the applicant starts, not what they end up with.

> **What to check**
> Is having two entry routes helpful or confusing? An institutional investor arriving at the home page needs to know which one applies to them. If the site does not make that obvious, record it in section 7 — it is the highest-traffic decision on the whole platform.
